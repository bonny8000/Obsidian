## Working Interpretation

AI can help with research operations and candidate issue discovery, but UX teams need explicit checks for reliability, validity, ethics, and method fit. Reflexive qualitative methods are a strong warning case: some research value comes from human interpretation itself.

Two concrete production examples from Korean product companies illustrate the "check tool" pattern: **Huribot** (Toss) reduces lightweight UT time from ~1 hour to seconds via image-based AI analysis; **Athena MCP** (Bucketplace) reduces UT setup time from 2 hours to 15 minutes via real-data prototype population. Both explicitly position AI as a supplement to, not replacement for, formal user research.

The SAGE vs. Lim tension maps a key fork in the field: SAGE argues AI is methodologically incongruent with Big Q reflexive approaches; Lim argues the problem is AI-as-analyst, not AI-as-thinking-partner. See [[concepts/ai-agents/ai-as-thinking-partner|AI as Thinking Partner]] and [[concepts/ux-research/genai-in-qualitative-research|GenAI in Qualitative Research]] for the conflict in full.

Bakhshi's "long accommodation" adds a profession-level quality gate: AI makes research generation cheaper, so the scarce work becomes proving that claims are valid and relevant to the decision being made.

The 2026 MeasuringU hallucination follow-up makes the AI review gate more concrete: AI-only usability problems should be sorted into genuine findings, false alarms, and hallucinations before they are treated as evidence. The quant UXR sources add a complementary learning path: build the basics of surveys, statistics, programming, and research design, then define rigor by decision usefulness and data quality rather than method complexity.
