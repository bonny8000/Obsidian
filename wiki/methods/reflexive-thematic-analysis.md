# Method: Reflexive Thematic Analysis

## Purpose

Reflexive thematic analysis develops themes through active researcher interpretation. It is useful when the goal is not just to count topics but to explain meaning, context, and tensions in qualitative evidence.

## Use When

- The data contains rich qualitative material.
- The research question needs interpretation and nuance.
- The team must understand tensions, framing, and meaning-making.

## Avoid When

- The team expects fully automated coding with no researcher judgment.
- The data is too thin for interpretive themes.
- The output must be a simple frequency count.

## Inputs

- Transcripts, notes, open-ended responses, or field observations.
- Researcher memoing process.
- Theme development criteria.
- Reflexive notes about assumptions and role.

## Procedure

1. Familiarize with the data before using summaries.
2. Generate initial codes.
3. Develop candidate themes.
4. Review themes against raw evidence.
5. Write themes as interpretive claims with evidence and caveats.

## Outputs

- Theme set with supporting evidence.
- Interpretive memo.
- Tensions and counterexamples.
- Implications for design or further research.

## Quality Bar

- Themes are not just topic labels.
- Counterexamples are retained.
- LLM assistance must not replace researcher reflexivity.

## LLM Assistance

- **Safe uses:** generating candidate codes, comparing excerpts, surfacing contradictions.
- **Risky uses:** final theme authority, quote invention, removing ambiguity.
- **Verification required:** theme definitions and all supporting evidence.
