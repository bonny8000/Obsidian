# Opportunity Cost of Cognitive Effort

## Summary
A theory proposing that the subjective cost of cognitive effort is fundamentally an economic opportunity cost. Because the brain's cognitive control architecture features processing bottlenecks (shared, limited-capacity resources to prevent crosstalk interference), dedicating resources to one specific task means sacrificing the potential rewards of alternative activities (like mind-wandering or pursuing a different goal).

## Key Claims
- **Multiplexing and Bottlenecks**: The brain shares computational resources (multiplexing) to facilitate learning and generalization. However, concurrent demands on shared representations cause catastrophic interference. Bottlenecks are an adaptive mechanism to restrict processing to one task at a time.
- **The Cost of Prioritization**: Effort is experienced as costly because prioritizing the selected task deprioritizes others. The cost is computed as the expected utility of the next-best alternative task.
- **Task Density**: As task complexity increases (e.g., from a 1-back to a 2-back working memory task), the density of bottlenecking per unit time increases, leaving even less bandwidth for alternative processes, thereby inflating the opportunity cost and the subjective feeling of effort.
