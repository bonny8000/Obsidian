# Information-Theoretic View of Effort

## Summary
A perspective proposing that cognitive effort costs arise from the computational burden of updating prior beliefs to posterior beliefs during inference and action selection. 

## Key Claims
- The computational (and metabolic) cost of making an inference or selecting an action is proportional to the reduction in relative entropy.
- This reduction is mathematically quantified as the **Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence** between the prior belief distribution and the posterior belief distribution.
- Tasks that require selecting an action from many equally likely possibilities (a widely spread prior distribution) entail a massive reduction in uncertainty, generating a large KL divergence. This large informational shift translates into a high subjective experience of cognitive effort.
- This framework bridges computational information processing with biological constraints, as the KL divergence approximates the actual energetic cost of the neural control inputs required for the inference.
