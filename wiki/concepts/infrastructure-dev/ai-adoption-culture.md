# AI Adoption Culture

## Summary

The organizational practices that turn AI from individual experiments into shared capability and infrastructure: protected exploration time, psychological safety to share half-finished work, visible peer proof, and deliberate capture of **individual discovery → organizational asset**.

## Why It Matters

Most AI value is lost at the org layer — people try tools privately and nothing compounds. Two 2026 cases show contrasting routes to org-wide capability, and both treat AI as **infrastructure that reshapes workflows**, not a point tool.

## Key Claims

- **Two complementary models:**
  - **Bottom-up culture (Toss "AI Surf Day"):** protected Friday time, ~200 self-organized clubs, weekly show-and-tell, and **142 peer-nominated evangelists** (selected for sharing, not seniority). Culture beats program; the deliberate move is discovery → reusable asset.
  - **Structured cohort (Hanwha Life × AX LABS PBL):** a 6-week project-based program where 20 non-developer domain experts build and *own* real agents, gated by boundary-setting and a real-data milestone.
- **Common principles:** protected time/structure; learn by shipping real projects, not lectures; capture and reuse what individuals discover; treat outputs as durable org assets, not throwaway demos.
- **Culture > program:** time allocation + psychological safety + visible proof that peers solved similar problems is what actually drives adoption.

## Conflicts & Caveats

> [!warning] Success-biased sources
> Both are employer-brand / vendor accounts with no failure rates or sustainment metrics. Bottom-up clubs risk sprawl without asset capture; structured cohorts are support-heavy and expensive to scale.
