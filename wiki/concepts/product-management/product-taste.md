# Product Taste

## Summary

Product taste is the judgment to decide what should be built, which user problems matter, and what experience feels right when many possible features are technically buildable. The Figma taste framing adds a design-craft layer: taste is a cultivated point of view expressed through care, critique, trade-off judgment, and repeated intentional choices.

## Why It Matters

The transcript frames product taste as a rising bottleneck in AI product work. If AI makes code easier to produce, teams can build many things; the harder question becomes which things deserve to exist.

## Key Claims

- Code becoming cheaper increases the value of deciding what to write.
- Product taste helps teams choose among many user requests, GitHub issues, and internal ideas.
- Taste includes knowing the right UX for a model's current capability, not just imagining a future smarter model.
- Engineers, PMs, and designers can all contribute product taste if they understand users and quality.
- Taste is not replaced by AI because generated output still needs discernment, empathy, creative energy, and final judgment.
- In AI-assisted design, accepting the first output as final is a weak-taste failure mode.
- **Named as *the* differentiator from AI**, developed through creative side projects ([[wiki/sources/designer-builder-collapse|Ozenc & Yousefi, 2026]]) — asserted without an operational definition, which is this concept's standing gap rather than that source's alone.
- **The clearest concrete instance in this vault of taste as the unautomated remainder:** in [[wiki/sources/naver-d2-ai-hackathon-nstake|NStake]], AI generated a complete UI in an hour around a cute character theme. It was wrong because the finance team's spreadsheets used **cell colour as meaning** — yellow, grey, pale blue signalled information state, not decoration — so what those users needed was familiarity, formality, and a sense that the numbers could be trusted. Reading that from the users' existing conventions is exactly the judgment AI did not supply, and it cost real time to correct.

## What taste is doing work for

Across the sources, "taste" consistently names the same residual: **knowing what the audience's existing conventions mean, and what they will read as trustworthy.** That is narrower and more testable than "discernment" — and it suggests the assessable version of taste is *evidence about the audience*, not aesthetic preference.

> [!warning] Still undefined operationally
> No source in this vault defines how taste is assessed, taught, or distinguished from familiarity with a domain. As convergence claims pile up ([[concepts/product-management/role-convergence|role convergence]] now spans PM, research, and design), "taste is the differentiator" is doing increasingly heavy load-bearing work on an undefined term.
