# Research Influence

## Summary
Research influence is the ability of UX research to shape product strategy, priorities, and the roadmap. It is distinct from research quality; high-quality research can have low influence if it is poorly timed, framed, or socialized.

## Why it matters
Without influence, research becomes a "footnote" in product development. Strategic influence ensures that user needs are built into the product roadmap rather than being treated as optional polish.

## Key Claims
- **Upstream Engagement:** Influence is highest during the discovery and framing phases, before solutions are committed to the roadmap.
- **Metric Alignment:** To influence decisions, research findings must be translated into the business metrics (KPIs) that stakeholders care about.
- **Recommendations over Findings:** Stakeholders often lack the time or expertise to interpret raw data; explicit, actionable recommendations increase the likelihood of adoption.
- **Relational Trust:** Influence is built over time through consistent, useful, and honest partnership with product and engineering teams.
- **Influence becomes the whole job when execution automates.** [[wiki/sources/when-research-gets-faster|Venkat (2026)]] argues that AI compressed the *middle* of research — transcription, coding, first-pass analysis — leaving the front (framing a decision-changing question) and back (making a finding own a decision) untouched. Since the middle was never the bottleneck, faster research **exposes** organizational dysfunction rather than fixing it.
- **Volume is not influence.** More studies do not improve decision quality; organizations already struggle to prioritize conflicting signals. When studies become nearly free, the scarce resource becomes **stakeholder attention and decision slots**.
- **The failure patterns are timing and framing, not rigor.** Venkat's examples: feature-preference testing after architecture was finalized; onboarding research where the causes were already known; trust studies ahead of an already-made launch decision. Each is methodologically sound and organizationally useless.
- **Track post-launch outcomes** so influence is demonstrable rather than asserted.
