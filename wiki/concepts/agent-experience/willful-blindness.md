# Willful Blindness

> [!abstract] Summary
> The active, strategic avoidance of explanatory information or logic by human decision-makers interacting with AI, driven by a desire to avoid moral discomfort, cognitive conflict, or financial penalty (e.g., loss of a performance bonus).

> [!important] Why it Matters
> It challenges the fundamental assumption that users naturally want transparency and explanation from AI systems (Explainable AI). When individual incentives reward outcomes rather than processes, or when explanations expose potential biases (such as race or gender discrimination), humans will intentionally decline transparency to maintain "plausible deniability" and offload liability to the algorithm.

## 📝 Key Claims
- **Humans are not perfectly rational Bayesian agents in AI interaction:** They are strategic, motivated, and choose to remain ignorant when it simplifies their decisions or preserves their bonuses.
- **Incentives suppress transparency-seeking:** Outcome-based financial incentives make operators nearly 20% more likely to reject explanations, prioritizing simple compliance over understanding.
- **Bias warning increases avoidance:** When operators suspect that an explanation might reveal discrimination (e.g., race or gender bias influencing a loan approval), avoidance of explanations increases by an additional 10+ percentage points.
- **Willful blindness devalues human judgment:** If operators are permitted to ignore the "why," they eventually stop questioning the AI, leading to cognitive offloading and training humans "to stop asking why."

## ⚖️ Conflicts & Caveats
> [!note] Overriding AI
> The study notes that when users *do* choose to look at explanations, they are 6% more likely to challenge the AI and override its recommendations. Thus, the barrier is not that explanations don't work, but that users strategically avoid viewing them in the first place.
