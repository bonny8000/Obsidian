# Companion Attachment & Dependency Risk

## Summary

The central tension of [[concepts/agent-experience/ai-companion|AI companion]] design: the engineered emotional/identity lock-in that drives retention and lifetime value is simultaneously a user-safety and dependency hazard. The same [[concepts/agent-experience/relationship-architecture|relationship-architecture]] levers that move a product up the business-model ladder are the ones that risk manufacturing unhealthy dependence.

## Why It Matters

The Kakao Ventures thesis treats "strongest customer lock-in" as the goal — but that phrase reads as a business win and a welfare risk at once. Without naming this tension explicitly, the companion-cluster concepts would describe only the monetization side of a mechanism that has a serious downside.

## Key Claims

- **Lock-in vs user welfare:** deep emotional/identity lock-in is the explicit design goal, and the very same mechanism is the dependency/manipulation risk in companion AI ([[sources/kakao-vc-ai-companion-relationship|Kakao Ventures, 2026]]).
- **Aspiration/worldview AI shades toward ideological capture:** monetizing "consistent belief representation" and building "shared-worldview emulation spaces" sits near persuasion/cult dynamics (the Way of the Future reference cuts both ways).
- **Authenticity gap:** nurturing "co-growth" is a designed narrative, not a real relationship — its value depends on users experiencing engineered intimacy as genuine.
- **Governance is unresolved:** as products are deliberately engineered for deeper lock-in, who sets and governs the safety/dependency boundary is an open question.
- **Dependency now has candidate measurement — and a wider scope than companions.** [[wiki/sources/measuringu-measuring-the-ux-of-ai|Sauro & Lewis (2026)]] propose *AI Dependency* as a 3-item construct ("I tend to accept answers from AI chatbots without verifying their accuracy"), and cite **66%** relying on AI output without verifying, with **56%** having made mistakes from uncritical acceptance. That reframes dependency as a mainstream property of ordinary assistant use, not a companion-product edge case. **The instrument is unvalidated** — see [[wiki/concepts/ux-research/ai-ux-measurement-constructs|AI UX Measurement Constructs]].
- **Personification is measurable without being desirable.** The same instrument proposes an *AI Personification* construct (6 items). A higher score is not self-evidently a better product — this concept and [[concepts/agent-experience/parasocial-relationship|Parasocial Relationship]] are the reason to read that construct as a risk signal rather than an achievement.
- **Dependency is monetizable, which is why it resists remedy.** [[wiki/sources/kakao-vc-ai-agent-advertising|Kakao Ventures (2026)]] shows non-verification is the conversion mechanism for conversational advertising: **61.2%** sponsored selection versus **22.4%** in search, and explicit labels reduce it only to **55.5%**. The lock-in critique this page makes about companion products generalizes to any ad-funded assistant. See [[wiki/analyses/2026-07-30-trust-measurement-and-monetization|the 2026-07-30 memo]].

## Conflicts & Caveats

> [!warning] Source argues the business case, not the safety case
> The anchor source is a VC thesis advocating deeper lock-in; it acknowledges but does not resolve the dependency hazard. This concept exists to keep the welfare counterweight visible. It cites no clinical or harm data — the risk is argued structurally, not measured.

## Open Questions

- Does self-reported AI Dependency predict *observed* non-verification? The measurement question that would make this concept testable rather than structural.
- Where is the boundary between healthy attachment and harmful dependency, and who decides?
- What product-side safeguards (usage friction, reality reminders, off-ramps) reduce dependency without destroying the product?
- How should regulators treat AI explicitly optimized for emotional/identity lock-in?
