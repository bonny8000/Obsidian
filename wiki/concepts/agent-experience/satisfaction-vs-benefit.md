# Satisfaction vs Benefit

> [!abstract] Summary
> The gap between how good an AI interaction *feels* and whether it actually *helps* — captured as a "feels good × helps" 2×2 used as an evaluation lens for AI products.

> [!important] Why it Matters
> Satisfaction and positive ratings are the easiest signals to collect and the easiest to over-trust. They can mask harm (over-reliance, declining wellbeing) or no real benefit — so measuring satisfaction alone can point AI products in the wrong direction.

## 📝 Key Claims
- Four quadrants: feels-good-&-helps; feels-good-but-doesn't-help (over-reliance, sycophancy); feels-bad-but-helps (confronting hard facts); feels-bad-&-doesn't-help (errors, hallucination, over-refusal).
- "Feeling better" can itself be the benefit (e.g., venting) — satisfaction is not always hollow.
- Backfire comes from accumulating uncritical acceptance of whatever sounds good, without verification or self-reflection.
- The key user variable is whether the boundary between AI and a person stays clear (metacognition).
- Short-term and long-term measurement can diverge — immediate satisfaction may not predict long-term help.

## ⚖️ Conflicts & Caveats
> [!warning] Contradictions
> Pushes back on satisfaction/CSAT-style metrics as a sufficient measure of AI value. The 2×2 is a framing tool from a practitioner synthesis, not a validated instrument; how to operationalize "benefit" is unresolved.
