# AI Qualitative Research Map (Five-Specification Framework)

> [!abstract] Summary
> A way to evaluate AI use in qualitative research *precisely* — by specifying five things rather than issuing a yes/no verdict: **stage** (where AI entered), **task type** (what it did), **method fit** (does it match the tradition), **output status** (what the output may count as), and **validation/traceability** (how it was checked).

> [!important] Why it Matters
> "AI in qualitative research" is not one practice — it names many distinct interventions (drafting guides, chatbot interviews, transcript cleaning, retrieval, codebook application, theme suggestion, second readings, quote selection). Debates look contradictory because studies collapse these. Specifying the five axes makes any AI-assisted study evaluable and disclosable.

## 📝 Key Claims
- **Stage:** before / during / after data collection, during analysis, during reporting — earlier entry shapes the *frame* or the *data*, raising the stakes.
- **Task type:** operational, retrieval, classification (strongest evidence), summarization, exploratory, comparative (AI as a *second reading* — most promising), interpretive (most unsettled), generative-of-data (AI interviewing changes the account).
- **Method fit:** deductive content analysis fits AI; reflexive thematic analysis is contested (themes are *developed* by positioned researchers, not discovered).
- **Output status ladder:** suggestion → working material → candidate analytic object → evidence pointer → draft claim → supported claim. Damage comes from treating candidates as findings.
- **Validation:** traceability to source, fit-with-output-type checks, *visible* human revision, comparison across readings, negative-case checking, documented limits. "Human in the loop" is too vague.
- Cross-cutting: descriptive matching ≠ interpretive analysis; outputs are situated (vary by model/prompt/data); validation is a *design problem*.

## ⚖️ Conflicts & Caveats
> [!warning] Contradictions
> Deliberately refuses a verdict — it won't tell you whether to use AI, only how to specify and evaluate a given use. It maps the literature rather than testing it.
