# Human Interpretation

## Summary

Human interpretation is the situated, reflective sense-making work humans perform when analyzing meaning, context, power, experience, and ambiguity.

## Why It Matters

In qualitative and UX research, not all analysis is extraction. Some analysis depends on the researcher's position, relationship to participants, and reflective judgment.

## Key Claims

- Human interpretation is central to reflexive qualitative approaches; researcher positionality is part of the method. *(SAGE 2025)*
- AI can assist with organization or pattern-surfacing, but may not replace method-required interpretive work. *(SAGE 2025)*
- Researchers own irreplaceable context: observed moments, stakeholder dynamics, what remained unspoken, and real-world constraints that AI cannot access. *(Lim 2026)*
- The boundary is not AI-yes vs. AI-no — it is AI-as-analyst (problematic) vs. AI-as-thinking-partner (compatible). *(Lim 2026)*
- Teams should distinguish mechanical analysis tasks from interpretive research work, and document which AI played. *(Both sources)*
- Scoping and reframing are interpretive acts: the researcher must read stakeholder context, hidden assumptions, and what the original request would make invisible. *(Bakhshi 2026)*
