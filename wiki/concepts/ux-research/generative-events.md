# Generative Events

## Summary

"Generative events" is the proposed basic *ontological unit of design* in a DRS2026 theory paper by Fengjin Yu and Qiaozhi Zhao (Loughborough). Rather than comparing Participatory Design and Co-Design method-by-method, the paper argues their practical overlap needs an ontological account, and supplies one by synthesising Object-Oriented Design Ontology (OODO) with Process Ontology: design is reframed as a dynamic process in which humans *and non-human objects* co-construct relationships and meaning. The shift is from "tool logic" to "event logic."

## Why It Matters

It offers a unified theoretical foundation for the PD/Co-Design fusion and a vocabulary for treating design as something that *happens* (an event of co-construction) rather than something a designer *does with tools*. That move opens space for non-human-centred methods and deepens the "design as research" claim — useful framing for anyone designing human–agent collaboration, where non-human actors genuinely participate in meaning-making.

## Key Claims

- **The generative event is the proposed basic ontological unit of design** — design theorised as a process of relational co-construction, not a sequence of tool applications ([[sources/drs2026-generative-events-design-ontology|Yu & Zhao, DRS2026]]).
- **Two ontological bases are synthesised:** *Object-Oriented Design Ontology (OODO)* — the design-research flavour of object-oriented ontology — and *Process Ontology* — a design-as-becoming/flux lens. The generative event is where the two meet.
- **From tool logic to event logic:** a methodological shift that yields *non-human-centred methods*, where non-human objects co-construct relationships and meaning rather than being passive instruments.
- **An ontological account of the PD / Co-Design overlap:** the paper claims the practical convergence of Participatory Design and Co-Design needs an ontological foundation, not just method comparison.
- **An "ontological deepening of design as research"** is named as a contribution — research-through-design grounded in what design fundamentally *is*.
