# The Research Engine

## Summary
The Research Engine is a multimodal operating model for UX research designed to match the accelerated decision velocity of AI-era product development. It replaces the traditional "one-size-fits-all" embedded research study with a tiered system that routes questions to the appropriate level of rigor and speed.

## Why It Matters
AI tools have made it incredibly cheap for product teams to generate design options and code, expanding the decision surface area. If research relies solely on traditional 3-6 week studies, the vast majority of product decisions will be made without evidence. The Research Engine allows research functions to maintain high coverage and speed without sacrificing foundational understanding.

## Key Claims
- The Engine consists of three operating modes:
  1. **[[wiki/concepts/ux-research/micro-research|Micro Research]]**: 24-72 hour AI-moderated studies for low-risk, low-ambiguity decisions.
  2. **[[wiki/concepts/ux-research/sprint-research|Sprint Research]]**: 1-2 week studies combining AI moderation with researcher-led follow-ups for moderate risk/ambiguity.
  3. **[[wiki/concepts/ux-research/deep-research|Deep Research]]**: Multi-week foundational investigations for high-risk, high-ambiguity spaces.
- **[[wiki/concepts/ux-research/the-frame|The Frame]]**: The foundational user model built by Deep Research. Micro and Sprint research operate *within* The Frame.
- Fast research answers questions; Deep research builds the context that determines *which* questions are worth asking. 
- Fast research acts as a trigger: when Micro or Sprint studies repeatedly surface contradictions or surprises, it signals that The Frame has decayed and Deep Research is needed.
