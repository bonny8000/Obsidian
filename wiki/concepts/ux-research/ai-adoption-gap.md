# AI Adoption Gap

> [!abstract] Summary
> The widening divergence in perception, expectations, and priorities between executive leadership (C-suite) and individual contributors (ICs) regarding the efficiency gains, workload, and value of AI tools deployed in an organization.

> [!important] Why it Matters
> When leadership assumes AI has multiplied team productivity far beyond reality, they may set unrealistic deadlines, cut budgets, or freeze headcounts. This mismatch creates organizational friction, contributor burnout, and can derail AI integration initiatives by fostering resistance.

## 📝 Key Claims
- **Significant perception mismatch on efficiency:** Survey data shows 83% of leaders believe AI tools have made their teams more efficient, compared to only 65% of individual contributors.
- **Divergence in perceived value:** 72% of C-suite leaders feel their organization relies more on research than it did a year ago, while only 44% of ICs share that view.
- **Conflicting priorities block alignment:** ICs are focused on day-to-day constraints (budgets, speed to insights, learning new methods), while leaders focus on macro outcomes (ROI communication, driving data-driven decisions, data source consolidation).

## ⚖️ Conflicts & Caveats
> [!warning] Measurement Bias
> Executive optimism is often driven by vendor promises and high-level productivity reports, whereas IC skepticism is grounded in "hidden work" (e.g., debugging AI outputs, prompt tweaking, manual data verification). True efficiency must be measured objectively rather than through surveys.
