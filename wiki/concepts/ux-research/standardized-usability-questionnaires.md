# Standardized Usability Questionnaires

## Summary

Psychometrically qualified instruments for measuring perceived usability. Post-study: SUS (10 items, 0–100), PSSUQ/CSUQ, SUPR-Q (websites), UMUX/UMUX-LITE. Post-task: SEQ (single item), SMEQ, ASQ. Quality is judged on reliability (Cronbach's alpha; ≥0.70 acceptable for research), validity, and sensitivity.

## Why it matters

Standardized instruments beat homegrown surveys: known reliability, comparability across products and time, and published norms. They are the cheapest credible quant metric a small team can adopt.

## Key claims

- Book's recommendation: **SUS post-study; SEQ or SMEQ post-task**; use 7 points for SEQ rather than 5 for better reliability. (Sauro & Lewis 2016, ch. 8; conf 0.95)
- SUS began as "quick and dirty" but psychometric studies show it is quick and *not* dirty. (conf 0.9)
- Reliability ≥0.90 is required only for high-stakes individual assessment; 0.70–0.80 suffices for UX research. (conf 0.9)
- Single-item post-task measures perform nearly as well as multi-item ones — simplicity wins at the task level. (conf 0.85)
