# Process Literacy

> [!abstract] Summary
> The skill of deliberately matching design-process depth to a problem's risk — knowing which process fits the job and the cost of not following it — rather than rigidly following or wholesale abandoning a process.

> [!important] Why it Matters
> Counters the popular "throw out the process, trust intuition, skip research, just build" narrative. As AI compresses workflows and agents begin to act and decide, *skipping the question of how the work maps to the problem becomes more costly, not less.*

## 📝 Key Claims
- What looks like "skipping the process" is usually process *compression* — running internalized stages faster; "intuition is really process, compressed and internalized."
- AI/vibecoding accelerates compression and democratizes it to less-experienced practitioners.
- Intuition does not replace process: it isn't viable for juniors, lacks accountability, fails in regulated/high-stakes domains, and carries bias.
- Solution-first design works only in narrow, mature contexts (and its showcased wins reflect survivorship bias); speed of execution doesn't remove the need for problem framing.
- The real skill is process literacy: match the process to the problem intentionally.
