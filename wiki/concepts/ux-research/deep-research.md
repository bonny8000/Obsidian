# Deep Research

## Summary
Deep Research is the foundational, high-investment tier of the [[wiki/concepts/ux-research/the-research-engine|Research Engine]]. It is used to explore highly ambiguous, high-risk problem spaces to build [[wiki/concepts/ux-research/the-frame|The Frame]]. It relies on extensive, researcher-led engagement and cannot be rushed or easily automated.

## Why It Matters
Without Deep Research, an organization's understanding of its users inevitably decays. Fast research methods (Micro and Sprint) assume a static user model. Deep Research is the only mechanism that safely updates that model when it breaks down. 

## Key Claims
- **Scope**: Used for high-risk, high-ambiguity, long-expiry questions. Typical use cases: entering a new market, mapping complex contextual workflows, resolving persistent contradictions surfaced by fast research.
- **Methodology**: 4 to 8 weeks of intensive exploration, often involving ethnography, diary studies, or in-depth foundational interviews. Researcher-led from start to finish.
- **Vulnerability**: Because Deep Research is slow and lacks immediate "quick wins" or two-day readouts, it is constantly at risk of being cannibalized by Micro and Sprint research requests.
- **Protection**: Deep Research requires structural protection:
  - **Calendar Blocking**: Schedule it proactively before tactical requests fill the calendar.
  - **Executive Sponsorship**: Tie it to a known strategic initiative.
  - **Budgeting**: Treat it as a fixed percentage of research capacity, not a residual.
- **Deliverable**: Not a typical readout, but an updated **Frame** (e.g., a new user journey, mental model artifact, or strategic problem statement).
