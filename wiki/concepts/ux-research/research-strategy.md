# Research Strategy

## Summary
A research strategy defines what a research practice will do—and not do—to deliver value to the organization. It acts as the "noun" or the blueprint that guides the "verb" of research operations.

## Why It Matters
Without a strategy, research practices often default to a "distributed model" where they respond piecemeal to stakeholder requests, making research look like an expensive and short-lived cost center. A strategy allows research to align with corporate priorities and amplify its value.

In an AI-assisted research environment, strategy should also define how the practice protects validity and decision relevance instead of only increasing artifact throughput.

## Key Characteristics of a Good Strategy
- **Prioritized:** Focuses on 3-4 key priorities.
- **Actionable:** Includes a practical plan of action.
- **Specific:** Addresses specific challenges and stakeholders.
- **Evolving:** Guided from the top but informed bottom-up by new learnings.
- **Simple & Shared:** Easy to understand and articulates a shared vision.
- **Framing-aware:** Separates requests that need procedural execution from wicked questions that require reframing before research begins.
- **Evidence-accountable:** Defines what claims research is allowed to make and what decisions those claims are meant to support.

## Strategic Orchestration
Towsey argues that to scale the value of research, a strategy must balance three things (see Figure 2.1 in [[sources/research-that-scales-towsey-2024|Research That Scales]]):
1. **People:** Attracting and growing talent.
2. **Systems:** Building the infrastructure to operate.
3. **Outputs:** Delivering high-quality insights.
