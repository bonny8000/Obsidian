# CTA Friction

## Summary
The psychological and cognitive resistance a user experiences before interacting with a Call to Action (CTA). Reducing CTA friction is a primary lever in conversion rate optimization and UX writing.

## Key Claims
- **Verb Weight**: The verb used in a CTA heavily influences perceived friction. "Heavy" verbs (e.g., "Buy", "Register", "Submit") imply a high commitment or cost. "Light" verbs (e.g., "Check", "See", "Explore") invite action with low perceived risk.
- **Uncertainty as Friction**: Ambiguity about what happens after clicking adds friction. Highly specific microcopy (e.g., "Enter your email to see pricing") reduces this uncertainty compared to generic copy (e.g., "Continue").
- **Trust Correlation**: Users with low brand trust are highly sensitive to CTA friction. Lowering the psychological barrier to entry is critical for initial funnel steps.
