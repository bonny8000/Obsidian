# PWDR (People Who Do Research)

## Summary
PWDR is an acronym coined by Kate Towsey (circa 2019) to describe the cohort of people who conduct research as part of their role but are not full-time research professionals. This includes designers, product managers, content strategists, and marketers.

## Why It Matters
In many organizations, research is democratized. While PWDRs are enthusiastic, they often lack the specialized skills of dedicated researchers. ResearchOps must support this cohort by providing:
- **Logistical Support:** "How-to" support (e.g., how to use a tool or access a participant).
- **Craft Support:** "What-to-do" support (e.g., which methodology to use or reviewing a discussion guide).

## Key Claims
- Supporting PWDRs requires a different operating model than supporting dedicated researchers.
- Craft support for PWDRs is hard to scale and can become an "energy sinkhole" if not managed through training, office hours, and playbooks.
- ResearchOps should aim to make research "painless" but not "cheap," ensuring quality is maintained even when democratized.
