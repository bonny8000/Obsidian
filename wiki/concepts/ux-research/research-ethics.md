# Research Ethics

## Summary

Research ethics concerns the responsibilities researchers have toward participants, communities, evidence, interpretation, privacy, consent, power, and downstream use. In ResearchOps, this is often framed as "Respect in Research."

## Why It Matters

AI-assisted research analysis can introduce risks around data handling, epistemic authority, bias, accountability, and environmental/social justice. Furthermore, as research scales, the risks and responsibilities scale multidimensionally, necessitating robust systems to ensure participants are cared for and data is handled legally and honestly.

## Key Frameworks

### Respect in Research Trifecta
Kate Towsey defines a synchronized approach to three equally important parts:
1. **Ethics:** How people (participants, researchers) are respected and cared for.
2. **Data Governance:** How personal data is collected, used, and safeguarded.
3. **Data Privacy:** Ensuring compliance with privacy laws (GDPR, CCPA, etc.).

## Key Claims

- **Legal Doesn't Mean Ethical:** Compliance with terms of service or privacy laws (e.g., the Facebook "emotional contagion" study) does not automatically make research ethical.
- **Productivity vs. Accountability:** Productivity gains (from AI or automation) do not automatically justify use; researcher accountability remains paramount.
- **Responsive, Not Mechanistic:** Ethics requires thoughtfulness and sensitivity, not just checklists. Protocols should be adjusted in response to global or social contexts (e.g., COVID-19).
