# Mental Models

## Summary
A mental model is the internal picture a user holds of how a system works — built from prior experience, culture, and language. Users act on their model, not on the system's actual implementation; when the two diverge, confusion and errors follow.

## Key Claims
- Psychology within cognitive science explains the motivations and expectations users bring to an interface — the raw material of mental models. *(Medium: Cognitive Science and UX)*
- Language and culture shape cognition (Sapir-Whorf hypothesis), so mental models differ across locales — icons, metaphors, and labels do not transfer universally. *(Medium: Cognitive Science and UX)*
- As interfaces move beyond point-and-click to AI, voice, and AR, users lack established models, making deliberate model-building (onboarding, feedback, transparency) more critical. *(Medium: Cognitive Science and UX)*

## Why It Matters (for UX)
Design succeeds when the interface's conceptual model matches the user's mental model. Research methods like card sorting, tree testing, and concept testing exist largely to surface that model before committing to structure.
