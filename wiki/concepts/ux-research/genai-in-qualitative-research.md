# GenAI in Qualitative Research

## Summary

GenAI in qualitative research is the use of generative AI to assist, automate, summarize, code, or interpret qualitative data. The SAGE position article argues that some reflexive qualitative methods should reject GenAI because they require human, positioned interpretation.

## Why It Matters

UX research often borrows qualitative methods. AI can speed up analysis, but method fit matters: not every interpretive practice can be reduced to text processing.

## Key Claims

- Some qualitative approaches are methodologically incompatible with GenAI *substitution*. *(SAGE 2025)*
- AI use in research should consider epistemic authority, ethics, justice, and environmental cost. *(SAGE 2025)*
- Automated thematic summarization is "the lowest-value task an AI can do" ??the issue is role, not presence. *(Lim 2026)*
- AI as a "structured thinking partner" (pressure-testing insights, not generating them) may be compatible with reflexive qualitative work. *(Lim 2026)*
- Quality of AI integration depends on the researcher's rigor; weak use creates false credibility. *(Lim 2026)*

## Conflicts / Caveats

The SAGE position and the Lim practitioner essay are partially in tension:
- SAGE: GenAI is methodologically incongruent with Big Q reflexive approaches because researcher positionality is intrinsic to the method.
- Lim: AI-as-analyst is incompatible, but AI-as-thinking-partner intensifies rather than undermines reflexivity.
The conflict may reduce to a definitional disagreement about what counts as "using AI for analysis." Do not merge these positions.
