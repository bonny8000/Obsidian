# Evidence Engineering

## Summary

Evidence engineering is the work that connects an analytical artifact (query, chart, model, forecast, classification) to the claim made from it: specifying the question, constructing the measure, designing the comparison, validating model-derived variables, preserving the analytical path, and stating conclusions at the level the design supports. Bakhshi proposes it as the durable core of quantitative practice as AI absorbs artifact production.

## Key Claims

- An analysis is an artifact; evidence is the artifact's connection to a claim. AI accelerates the former and leaves the latter as human responsibility. *(Bakhshi 2026)*
- Benchmarks (Spider 2.0, StatQA, BLADE) show systems produce executable analyses while capturing only a fraction of expert analytical decisions — the gap evidence engineering fills. *(Bakhshi 2026)*
- Validation should be built into recurring workflows (reference datasets, logging checks, visible analytical paths) rather than a specialist hand-reviewing every output. *(Bakhshi 2026)*
- "As more people become capable of producing an analysis, quantitative expertise becomes less about access to the tools and more about responsibility for what the analysis is allowed to mean." *(Bakhshi 2026)*

## Why It Matters (for UX)

This is the quantitative mirror of the vault's qualitative thread ([[wiki/concepts/ux-research/human-interpretation|Human Interpretation]], [[wiki/concepts/ux-research/ai-analysis|AI Analysis]]): in both, AI moves the human role from producing outputs to owning what outputs mean. For UX teams adopting AI analytics, it defines what to keep in-house even when dashboards write themselves.
