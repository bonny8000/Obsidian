# Quant UXR Learning Path

## Summary

A quant UXR learning path is a structured way to build quantitative research capability across statistics, surveys, programming, and research design.

## Why It Matters

Quant UXR can feel like a vague bundle of math, tools, and product judgment. Pearson's four-pillar model makes the learning problem more practical: identify the weakest pillar, choose resources for that pillar, and apply the skill to real product or research questions.

## Key Claims

- The four practical learning pillars are statistics, surveys, programming, and research design.
- Surveys are central because many quant UXR problems depend on sampling, wording, measurement, and response interpretation.
- Research design prevents the common failure of using a technically correct method for the wrong question.
- Programming should be learned through concrete work such as data cleaning, visualization, SQL querying, or confidence-interval plotting.
- Quant UXR beginners do not need to start with machine learning, Bayesian modeling, or advanced causal methods.
- Source collections such as QuantUX Blog, MeasuringU, Conjointly, and the Meta Research archive are useful when mapped to specific learning gaps.
