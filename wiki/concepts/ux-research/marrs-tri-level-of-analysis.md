# Marr's Tri-Level of Analysis

## Summary
A foundational framework in cognitive science proposed by neuroscientist David Marr, which posits that complex information processing systems (like human vision or, by extension, human cognition) must be understood at three distinct, complementary levels.

## Key Claims
- **Computational Level**: Specifies the *goals* of the computation. What does the system do? Why does it do these things? (e.g., What problems does it solve?)
- **Representation and Algorithmic Level**: Specifies the *methods*. How does the system do what it does? What representations does it use, and what algorithms manipulate those representations?
- **Implementation Level**: Specifies the *physical realization*. How are the representations and algorithms physically implemented (e.g., in the brain's neural networks or a computer's hardware)?

## Why It Matters (for UX)
Understanding users solely through behavioral analytics (Implementation/Algorithmic) often misses the overarching goal (Computational). By applying Marr's model, UX professionals can ensure they are designing not just for surface-level clicks, but for the user's deeper cognitive processes and goals.
