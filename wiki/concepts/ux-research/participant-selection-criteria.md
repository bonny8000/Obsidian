# Participant Selection Criteria

## Summary

Participant selection criteria are the rules used to decide who should be included in, excluded from, and balanced within a UX research sample.

## Why It Matters

Recruiting the wrong participant can look harmless during fieldwork but quietly distort synthesis, prioritization, and product decisions. Screening needs to describe the target user's actual behavior and experience, not only demographics or job titles.

## Key Claims

- Inclusion criteria should focus on behaviors, experiences, and context that directly connect to the research question.
- Exclusion criteria should remove people who qualify on paper but would distort the study, such as professional respondents, people with conflicting expertise, or participants who fail quality checks.
- Diversity criteria should be planned as a matrix so one group does not dominate the sample and get mistaken for the whole user base.
- Demographic filters can be useful, but they are insufficient when people with the same demographic profile have different mental models and lived experiences.
- AI-generated screeners should be checked against inclusion, exclusion, and diversity criteria before recruiting starts.
