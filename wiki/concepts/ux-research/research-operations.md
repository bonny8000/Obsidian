# Research Operations (ResearchOps)

## Summary
Research Operations (ResearchOps) is the specialized function that provides the "power to act" for a research organization. It involves the design and management of people, mechanisms, and strategies that enable researchers to focus on their craft and deliver measurable value to the business.

## Why It Matters
As research organizations scale, the complexity of logistics (recruitment, tools, data management) grows multidimensionally. Without a dedicated operations function, researchers become overwhelmed by administrative tasks, and the quality and impact of research decline.

## Key Claims
- **Research Does Not Scale, Systems Do:** Scaling is not just about hiring more researchers; it's about building predictable, proportional, and measured systems.
- **The Research Metropolis:** A mature research operating system is analogous to a well-planned city (like the London Underground), where infrastructure empowers unique, independent journeys.
- **Noun vs. Verb:** A research strategy is a noun (the plan), while research operations is a verb (the constant doing and execution).
- Participant recruiting quality depends on operational rules for inclusion, exclusion, and diversity, not only on sourcing volume.
- ResearchOps can scale volume without scaling research authority if it only optimizes logistics; mature operations should also build validity and decision-relevance infrastructure.
