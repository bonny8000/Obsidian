# Heuristics and Biases

## Summary
Heuristics are the mental shortcuts people use to decide quickly under limited attention; biases are the systematic errors those shortcuts produce. Both shape how users perceive, judge, and act in an interface — and how researchers can misread their own data.

## Key Claims
- Psychology's account of heuristics and biases is one of cognitive science's most direct contributions to UX: it explains non-rational but predictable user behavior. *(Medium: Cognitive Science and UX)*
- Bias operates on both sides of research — participants answer through shortcuts (satisficing, social desirability), and researchers interpret through their own (confirmation bias). *(Medium: Cognitive Science and UX)*
- Over-reliance on real-time behavioral analytics without theory invites biased interpretation: the numbers show what happened, not the shortcut that produced it. *(Medium: Cognitive Science and UX)*

## Why It Matters (for UX)
Designing with heuristics (defaults, anchors, framing) is powerful and easily crosses into dark patterns — the same mechanism that helps users decide can manipulate them. Research practice also needs explicit debiasing: triangulation, preregistered questions, and separating evidence from interpretation.
