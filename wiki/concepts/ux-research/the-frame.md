# The Frame

## Summary
The Frame is an organization's accumulated, actively maintained model of its users. It is not a static persona document or a research repository; it is a continuously updated representation of what the organization currently believes to be true about user context, workflows, mental models, and motivations based on the best available evidence.

## Why It Matters
Without a Frame, fast research (like [[wiki/concepts/ux-research/micro-research|Micro Research]]) tests artifacts in a vacuum. A team might perfectly optimize a pricing page that solves a problem the user doesn't actually have. The Frame provides the foundational context that makes fast, tactical research meaningful.

## Key Claims
- The Frame is built and rebuilt via **[[wiki/concepts/ux-research/deep-research|Deep Research]]**.
- **[[wiki/concepts/ux-research/micro-research|Micro]]** and **[[wiki/concepts/ux-research/sprint-research|Sprint Research]]** operate *within* The Frame, relying on its assumptions.
- Fast research provides signals about The Frame's decay. If quick studies surface persistent confusion or unexpected mental models, The Frame is breaking and must be rebuilt.
- A healthy Frame has four assessable properties:
  1. **Coverage**: Which user segments and problem spaces are mapped vs. assumed?
  2. **Freshness**: When was the underlying evidence generated?
  3. **Confidence**: Is the belief based on direct evidence or organizational folklore?
  4. **Ownership**: Who is explicitly accountable for keeping this area of the Frame current?
- The Frame should generate artifacts (like Belief Maps or Coverage Matrices) that plug directly into the tools where PMs and designers already make decisions (e.g., Notion, Jira).
