# RKM Model (Research Knowledge Management)

## Summary
The RKM Model is a framework for planning research knowledge management across the research workflow. It integrates theories like the SECI model and the DIKW hierarchy into a pragmatic application for user research.

## The Two Distinct Phases
1. **Communities of Practice ("Campfires"):**
   - **Vibe:** Bohemian, tight-knit, self-managed.
   - **Focus:** Socialization and Externalization (SECI).
   - **Output:** Conversations, messy notes, raw data.
   - **Tactics:** Pathways to observe research, analysis spaces, and tools.
2. **Knowledge Services ("Libraries" & "Repositories"):**
   - **Vibe:** Centrally managed, strongly governed.
   - **Focus:** Combination and Internalization (SECI).
   - **Output:** Research reports, literature reviews, curated recordings.
   - **Tactics:** Structured taxonomies, self-service libraries, referencing services.

## Key Principles
- **A tool alone will not deliver KM:** Strategy and skilled people (like librarians) are essential.
- **Findability predicates trust:** If people can't find research, they doubt its accuracy and relevance.
- **Calculate accumulation:** Plan for how much content will be maintained and archived over several years.
