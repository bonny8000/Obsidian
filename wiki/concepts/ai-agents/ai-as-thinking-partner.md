# AI as Thinking Partner

## Summary

AI as thinking partner is a framing of AI's role in qualitative research (and analysis more broadly) where AI acts as a structured sparring partner, pressure-tester, and organizer — rather than as an analyst that generates findings independently.

## Why It Matters

The dominant critique of AI in qualitative research (e.g., [[sources/sage-10778004251401851-genai-reflexive-qualitative-research|SAGE 2025]]) targets AI-as-analyst: AI replacing the interpretive work that reflexive methods require humans to do. This concept describes a compatible alternative role where AI augments rather than replaces human interpretation.

For UX researchers, it reframes the question from "can AI do this?" to "what is AI doing in this workflow, and does that role fit the method?"

## Key Claims

- AI should be used to pressure-test insights against evidence, not to generate initial findings. *(Lim 2026)*
- Automated thematic summarization is "the lowest-value task an AI can do" in qualitative research. *(Lim 2026)*
- The thinking-partner role forces the researcher to articulate reasoning more clearly, which intensifies rather than bypasses reflexivity. *(Lim 2026)*
- Weak use (dumping raw data for AI to summarize) creates false credibility without analytical substance. *(Lim 2026)*
- Strong use demands rigorous input, interrogation of outputs, and researcher ownership of claims. *(Lim 2026)*

## Conflicts / Caveats

- The SAGE position article argues that even "assistant" AI roles may be methodologically incongruent with Big Q qualitative approaches, because the researcher's positionality is part of the method itself. Lim's practitioner view is more permissive. Confidence in the thinking-partner framing: 0.75 (one practitioner source, not peer-reviewed).
- The line between "pressure-testing" and "generating findings" is researcher-dependent and not procedurally defined by Lim.
