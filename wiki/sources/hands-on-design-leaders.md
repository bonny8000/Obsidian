# The market wants its hands-on leaders back

## Citation

UX Mentor Diaries, “The market wants its hands-on leaders back,” retrieved 2026-07-27. [Original article](https://uxmentor.substack.com/p/the-market-wants-its-hands-on-leaders)

## Source Type

Design-leadership commentary about player-coach leadership.

## Location

`raw/web/2026-07-27-hands-on-design-leaders.md`

## Summary

The article argues that organizations want leaders who can still engage with real design work while developing people and delivering through a team. The desired profile is neither a pure super-IC nor a detached manager, but a leader who can move between craft, coaching, and business outcomes.

## Key Claims

- Hands-on fluency helps leaders unblock teams and make credible quality decisions.
- Leadership success is measured by what happens through other people, not only by personal output.
- The player-coach role must be bounded; it is not permission to micromanage or take work back.

## Useful Examples

- Drop into a file or workflow to remove a concrete blocker, then return ownership to the team.
- Pair craft review with coaching and outcome review rather than treating them as separate jobs.

## Constraints / Caveats

This is advice and commentary, not evidence of a broad labor-market trend.

## Design Implications

Leadership roles should define how much craft participation, coaching, and strategic accountability are expected, with explicit anti-micromanagement boundaries.

## Tensions

Maintaining craft credibility can conflict with delegation, capacity, and the need to build independent teams.

## Open Questions

- What level of hands-on work preserves credibility without becoming a bottleneck?
- Which leadership outcomes should be used to evaluate a player-coach design leader?
