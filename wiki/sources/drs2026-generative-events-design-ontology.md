## Summary

This DRS2026 theory paper addresses a problem felt in practice but under-theorised: **Participatory Design (PD)** and **Co-Design** increasingly overlap in method, yet the literature explains the overlap mostly by *comparing methods* rather than by accounting for *why* the two integrate. Yu and Zhao argue the missing piece is **ontological** — a claim about what design fundamentally *is*. Their proposal is to treat the **"generative event"** as the **basic ontological unit of design**, a construct built by **synthesising Object-Oriented Design Ontology (OODO) with Process Ontology**. On this view design is not the wielding of tools by a human subject on passive objects, but a **dynamic generative process in which humans and non-human objects together construct relationships and meaning within a specific context**. The headline move is a **methodological paradigm shift from a "tool logic" to an "event logic,"** which the authors say yields **non-human-centred methods** and an **ontological deepening of "design as research."** The contribution is offered as a **unified theoretical foundation** for the PD/Co-Design fusion.

## Key Claims

- **The PD↔Co-Design overlap is real but under-explained.** Existing research is "largely confined to method comparisons" and "lacks an ontological perspective capable of explaining its underlying integrative mechanisms." (Verbatim.)
- **The fix is ontological, not methodological-comparative.** The paper "argues for a fundamental ontological shift" — changing the unit of analysis rather than re-tabulating methods.
- **"Generative events" are proposed as the basic ontological unit of design.** Constructed by "synthesising Object-Oriented Design Ontology (OODO) with Process Ontology." (Verbatim.)
- **Design is reframed as relational and more-than-human.** "Humans and non-human objects collaboratively construct relationships and meaning within specific contexts" — objects are co-constitutive participants, not inert means.
- **Methodological thesis: shift from "tool logic" to "event logic."** This is named as the paper's "crucial methodological paradigm shift."
- **Claimed outputs:** (1) a *unified theoretical foundation* for the fusion of PD and Co-Design; (2) *non-human-centred methods*; (3) an *ontological deepening of "design as research."*
- **Keywords:** Participatory Design; Co-Design; Design Ontology.

## Design Implications

- Offers a theoretical warrant for **Participatory Design** and **Co-Design** practitioners to stop arguing over *which method* and instead design around the [[concepts/ux-research/generative-events|generative event]] — the situated moment where people and materials/tools/artefacts jointly produce meaning. Plan sessions as event-staging, not tool-deployment.
- Supplies vocabulary for **more-than-human / non-human-centred design**: treat materials, prototypes, AI systems, and environment as **co-constitutive participants** in research, not passive instruments — relevant to studying agentic systems where the "object" acts back.
- Reinforces **design as research** / research-through-design (cf. [[concepts/ux-research/research-methods-foundations|research methods foundations]]): if design *is* a generative process producing relationships and meaning, the act of designing is itself knowledge-production, not just application.
- "Event logic" is a useful reframe for [[concepts/agent-experience/collaboration-patterns|human–agent collaboration]] research — model the interaction as a co-constructed event between human and non-human agent rather than a tool the human operates.

## Tensions

- **Object-Oriented Ontology vs. Process Ontology** are not obviously compatible — OOO foregrounds discrete withdrawn objects; process philosophy foregrounds becoming/flux. *How* the paper reconciles them into "generative events" is the crux and is not visible in the abstract.
- **"Non-human-centred" vs. the participatory tradition's human emancipatory roots.** PD historically centres marginalised *human* stakeholders (Scandinavian workplace-democracy lineage); de-centring the human risks tension with PD's political commitments — the abstract does not address this.
- **Unifying theory vs. methodological pluralism.** A single "unified foundation" may flatten the deliberate diversity that distinguishes PD from Co-Design in the first place.

## Open Questions

- How exactly is OODO synthesised with Process Ontology to yield a "generative event"? What are an event's constituents and boundaries?
- What do the promised **"non-human-centred methods"** look like operationally — new workshop formats, analysis units, documentation practices?
- Does "event logic" generate testable or teachable practice, or is it primarily a reframing lens?
- How does this map onto AI/agent design, where the "non-human object" is itself generative? (The paper predates wide agent-UX framing but the construct seems portable.)
