## Summary

The article argues that UX research weakened its disciplinary authority through a long pattern of organizational accommodation. Bakhshi names two standards that should form the spine of serious industry research: validity, or whether evidence supports a claim, and decision relevance, or whether the research answers the organizational decision at hand.

The essay traces several historical moments where the field could have consolidated around these standards but did not: HCI method translation without its validity substrate, design thinking's legitimacy tradeoff, data science's stronger experimentation feedback loop, academic imports that were rigorous but not calibrated to product decisions, ResearchOps scaling logistics more than validity infrastructure, hypergrowth making weak standards visible, influence language shifting attention to persuasion, and AI making artifact generation cheap.

## Extracted Claims

- Validity and decision relevance should be the core accountability standards for industry research.
- Research authority erodes when methods become techniques detached from claim-support rules.
- A research practice can produce persuasive artifacts while still failing to answer the right decision or support the claims it makes.
- ResearchOps can scale volume without strengthening authority if it does not build evidence-quality and decision-fit infrastructure.
- AI increases the need for explicit research accountability because it makes interviews, summaries, reports, and other artifacts cheaper to generate.
