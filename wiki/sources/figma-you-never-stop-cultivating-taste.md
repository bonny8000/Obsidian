# Figma: You Never Stop Cultivating Taste

## Citation

Crisan, Loredana. "You Never Stop Cultivating Taste." Figma Blog, 2026-06-08.

URL: https://www.figma.com/blog/you-never-stop-cultivating-taste/

Raw source card: `raw/web/figma-you-never-stop-cultivating-taste-2026-06-12.md`

## Summary

Figma CDO Loredana Crisan argues that craft mastery depends on cultivating a point of view, not just learning tools. AI can help designers explore more broadly and render intent faster, but it cannot replace taste. Taste is built through practice, critique, empathy, and repeated intentional choices.

The article is useful for the wiki's product-taste and AI-design themes because it frames taste as care: care for craft, users, details, and the final result.

## Key Claims

- Expertise is not only skill acquisition; it is also a cultivated way of seeing.
- Taste is a point of view plus the skill to express it.
- Product design taste appears in trade-offs: form vs. function, expressivity vs. legibility, what to add, and what to remove.
- Taste develops through practice, mentors, critique, feedback, and collaboration.
- Hiring for taste can look for discernment, empathy, and creative energy.
- AI can expand exploration and execution range, but the vision and final judgment still come from the designer.
- Accepting the first AI output as final is incompatible with strong craft judgment.

## Useful Examples

- Music composition and sound engineering as analogies for design taste.
- Dieter Rams' Braun work as an example of carrying intent through context and detail.
- James Dyson's prototype iteration as an example of not accepting early output as final.

## Constraints / Caveats

- This is a design-leadership essay, not a research study.
- It is strongest as a framing source for craft, critique, and AI-era judgment, not as empirical evidence.
- The article reflects Figma leadership's design philosophy and should be paired with portfolio evidence when used for hiring or evaluation rubrics.

## Design Implications

- AI-era design workflows should keep human critique and taste as explicit review gates.
- Design evaluation rubrics should test whether a designer can explain trade-offs and user impact, not only whether the artifact looks polished.
- For AI-generated product work, require iteration beyond first output and evidence of intentional choices.
- Connect this source to project reviews where the hard question is what is worth shipping, not whether something can be generated.

## Tensions

- AI can increase creative range while also tempting teams to accept surface-level polish.
- Taste is particular and subjective, but product work still needs shared quality language.
- Tool fluency can help craft, but it can also hide weak judgment.
