## Summary

The most structured framework in the vault for reasoning about AI in qualitative research. Core move: **"AI in qualitative research" is not one practice** — it names many distinct interventions (drafting guides, translating, chatbot interviews, transcript cleaning, retrieval, codebook application, theme suggestion, second readings, drafting findings, quote selection, disclosure language). So "Does AI work in qual research?" is the wrong question, and the literature only *looks* contradictory because studies ask different questions. Bakhshi offers a **five-axis specification** to evaluate any AI-assisted qualitative work precisely, plus a practical disclosure checklist.

## Key Claims

- **The five specifications** (the heart of the piece):
  1. **Stage** — where AI entered: before / during / after data collection, during analysis, during reporting. Earlier entry shapes the *frame* or the *data*, not just the analysis → higher stakes.
  2. **Task type** — operational, retrieval, **classification** (strongest evidence: deductive codebook application ≈ human agreement), summarization, exploratory, **comparative** (AI as a *second reading* — most promising), **interpretive** (most unsettled), **generative-of-data** (AI interviewing — changes the account, not just analysis).
  3. **Method fit** — deductive content analysis fits AI well; **reflexive thematic analysis is contested** (themes are *developed* by positioned, reflexive researchers, not discovered — Jowsey/Braun/Clarke + 414 signatories argue GenAI isn't congruent); grounded theory, discourse analysis, phenomenology, ethnography, participatory each carry different assumptions.
  4. **Output status** — what the output is *allowed to count as*: suggestion → working material → candidate analytic object → evidence pointer → draft claim → supported claim. Much damage = treating candidate outputs as findings.
  5. **Validation & traceability** — traceability to source; fit-with-output-type checks; **visible** human revision; comparison across readings; **negative-case checking**; documentation of limits. "Human in the loop" is too vague unless you can see what the human actually did.
- **What the literature suggests:** AI is most reliable for *bounded, checkable* tasks (deductive coding); **descriptive matching ≠ interpretive analysis**; AI as a **second reading** is the most promising use; outputs vary by model/prompt/data/workflow (situated, not stable facts); AI-mediated interviews produce a *different kind of account* and are under-studied; validation is becoming a *design problem*.
- Practical **disclosure checklist** (12 questions): what AI was used for, where, what task, what method, output status, traceability, what humans changed, **what was NOT delegated**, model conditions, what claim is justified, limits.

## Useful Examples

- **Second-reading use:** a multimorbidity study comparing human reflexive TA with Claude-Sonnet-assisted analysis, examining convergence/divergence/framing shifts.
- **Deductive coding evidence:** a 2023 LLM content-analysis study (GPT-3.5 ≈ human agreement) and a 2026 *Implementation Science Communications* GPT-4o deductive-coding workflow.
- **Output-status ladder** and the **5-spec map** are directly reusable as evaluation rubrics for any AI-assisted qual paper or internal study.

## Constraints / Caveats

- Practitioner synthesis (June 2026 snapshot), not original empirical work; it *maps* the literature rather than testing it.
- Deliberately refuses a yes/no verdict — its value is precision, not a recommendation to use or avoid AI.
- Deep, long, methodological; the raw capture compresses it — verify specific cited studies before quoting.

## Design Implications

- Make the **five specifications** a standard internal template for any AI-assisted research write-up — it converts "we used AI" into something reviewers can actually evaluate.
- Default to **bounded/checkable** AI tasks (retrieval, deductive coding, summaries-as-orientation) and treat interpretive/theme outputs as **candidates**, never findings, until grounded in source + negative cases.
- Favor **AI-as-second-reading** over AI-as-replacement — the value is making analytic assumptions and omissions visible.
- Operationalizes the vault's recurring "surface good ≠ truly good" theme for qual work: fluent interpretive prose ≠ a grounded reading.

## Tensions

- Sits between the optimistic "AI scales qual" camp and the SAGE-paper "reject GenAI in reflexive coding" camp — argues *both* are under-specified.

## LLM Use

- **Use for:** evaluating/designing AI-assisted qualitative studies with precision (stage/task/method-fit/output-status/validation); writing or auditing an AI-disclosure section; deciding which qual tasks are safe to delegate; arguing why "does AI work in qual?" is the wrong question.
- **Do not use for:** a verdict on whether to use AI (it refuses one); citing the referenced studies' results without checking the originals.
- **Best prompt pattern:** "Map this AI-assisted qual study on Bakhshi's five axes (stage, task type, method fit, output status, validation). For each, state what's adequate and what's missing, then write the disclosure paragraph a reader would need."
