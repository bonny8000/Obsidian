# What AI-use disclosure should be included in UX research reports?

## Short Answer
UX research reports that involved AI should disclose: (1) which tasks AI assisted with (transcription, coding, summarization, analysis); (2) which model or tool was used; (3) how AI output was validated or reviewed by a human researcher; and (4) whether the AI role affected the methodological claims being made. For reflexive qualitative work, a fifth disclosure is needed: whether the researcher's positionality and interpretation remained primary.

## Evidence
- [[concepts/ux-research/research-ethics|Research Ethics]] — "Researcher accountability remains necessary when AI is involved. Productivity gains do not automatically justify AI use in research." Disclosure is the operational expression of accountability.
- [[concepts/ux-research/methodological-integrity|Methodological Integrity]] — "Method fit matters more than tool novelty. Reliability and validity checks are part of integrity in usability analysis."
- [[concepts/ux-research/genai-in-qualitative-research|GenAI in Qualitative Research]] — "AI use in research should consider epistemic authority, ethics, justice, and environmental cost." Disclosure enables readers to apply these lenses.
- [[sources/sage-10778004251401851-genai-reflexive-qualitative-research|SAGE: Rejecting GenAI for Reflexive Qualitative Research]] — The position paper's concern with epistemic authority implies that readers need to know where AI was used to evaluate the authority of claims.
