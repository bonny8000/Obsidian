## Extracted Claims

- AI-native product teams need much shorter feedback loops than traditional product teams.
- PMs remain valuable, but their work shifts toward goal clarity, product taste, prioritization, launch systems, evals, and cross-functional unblockers.
- Code becoming cheaper increases the value of deciding what should be built.
- Research Preview can reduce launch friction by setting user expectations that a feature is early and may change.
- Team principles and metrics can replace some heavy PRD work by enabling decentralized decisions.
- Engineers with product taste and PMs/designers who can ship code reduce handoff overhead.
- The cost of shipping many AI features quickly is product consistency and user education.
- AI product onboarding becomes more important when tools change faster than users can track.
- Agentic products should be designed for the current model's strengths and weaknesses, not only an imagined future AGI model.
- Product managers can help by writing focused evals that define what success looks like.
- Model harnesses and prompts should be revisited as model capabilities improve because some scaffolding becomes unnecessary.
- Cowork-style agents are useful when the output is not code: decks, docs, meeting prep, customer briefs, email/Slack workflows, and synthesis.
- Reliable automation requires pushing beyond 90-95% accuracy; otherwise the human still cannot fully trust it.
