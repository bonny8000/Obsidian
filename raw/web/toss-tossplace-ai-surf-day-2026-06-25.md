## Summary

TossPlace frames AI not as a point tool but as **organizational infrastructure that changes work flows and business structure**. The flagship mechanism is **AI Surf Day** — every Friday (April→June), team members step off their Mon–Thu work to experiment with AI and apply it to real tasks. Named after Jon Kabat-Zinn's line, "You can't stop the waves, but you can learn to surf": rapid AI change can't be halted, only navigated. The strategic move is to convert **individual discovery → organizational asset**.

## Key Points

- **AI as infrastructure, not a tool.** The stated goal is to redesign workflows and business structure, not just add productivity tips.
- **AI Surf Day cadence:** dedicated Friday time (Apr–Jun) for AI experimentation and application to real work; protected exploration time is treated as the precondition for adoption.
- **AI Surf Club:** ~**200 self-organized groups** by role/interest. Examples: security engineers cataloging AI antipatterns; data engineers building shared knowledge wikis; a team shipping a complaint-monitoring portal within ~1 month; cross-functional marketing teams creating reusable AI assets.
- **AI Surf Weekly:** sessions sharing internal success stories/lessons so teams can reuse what peers already prototyped (cross-silo knowledge transfer).
- **AI Surf Evangelist program:** **142 employees** across Toss subsidiaries, selected by **peer nomination** for enthusiasm/sharing (not technical seniority); they run workshops, propose case studies, and champion adoption.
- **Culture over program.** Success hinges on time allocation, psychological safety to share half-finished work, and *visible proof that peers solved similar problems* — not on the formal structure alone. Quote (participant): "The greatest gain wasn't improved AI skills but the confidence that 'we can do this.'"
- **Discovery → asset:** individual methods naturally spread into team workflows and new problem-solving approaches; the org captures the diffusion deliberately.
