## Summary

The article argues that AI is separating research into two kinds of work: procedural work and wicked work. Procedural work includes repeatable tasks with clear inputs and outputs, such as transcription, clustering, summarization, formatting, and standardized synthesis. Wicked work includes scoping, reframing stakeholder requests, identifying whether a method will mislead, deciding when evidence is actionable, reading what participants do not say, and absorbing the social cost of pushing back on a flawed framing.

The core claim is that AI will absorb the tame, procedural parts fastest, making the wicked parts more visible and more central to research seniority. Research evaluation systems that count studies shipped, methods used, or artifacts produced may underrate the work that prevents the wrong study from being run.

## Extracted Claims

- Research work contains both tame procedural tasks and wicked scoping/interpretive tasks.
- AI is strongest at bounded, repeatable, verifiable research tasks after the question is well formed.
- Scoping is wicked because stakeholder requests are rarely clean, framing is never neutral, and defining the question changes the problem.
- Good scoping often leaves no visible artifact because its value may be preventing the wrong study from happening.
- Research seniority should be evaluated by framing judgment, method-fit judgment, moderation judgment, and the ability to absorb social costs, not only by method breadth or study volume.
