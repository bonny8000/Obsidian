# AI as a Senior Hire, Not an Intern — Design Meets AI

## Summary

An interview arguing that the dominant "AI as an army of interns" metaphor is the wrong management model. Holbrook's claim is that these systems produce better work when given **broad intentions and room to solve**, the way you would brief an experienced colleague, rather than the micro-specified task lists you would hand a junior. The piece doubles as a one-year retrospective on Holbrook's earlier "primitives" framework for AI interaction, scoring which primitives landed and which did not.

## Key Points

- **The core reframe:** manage AI like senior staff — "imbue it with intentions" instead of prescribing steps. Detailed instruction suppresses the creative problem-solving that makes the systems useful.
- **Why "intern" misleads:** it misrepresents where the systems are strong, and it quietly devalues human expertise by implying the human's job is supervision of the unskilled.
- **Discontinuous capability jumps:** an abrupt step-change in coding model performance around December produced a qualitative, not incremental, shift in what was possible.
- **Adoption model that worked:** bottom-up experimentation made *expected but not mandatory*, with leadership responsible for pulling promising tools into real iteration cycles. Psychological safety was engineered partly through absurdist naming ("Royal Academy of AI") to lower the cost of looking uninformed.

### The primitives, one year on

| Primitive | Holbrook's one-year read |
|---|---|
| Chat with everything | Partially realized; still centralized |
| Semantic resize | Mainstream, but underused for accessibility |
| Remix | Expanding via style-transfer models |
| Format translation | Underdeveloped despite education potential |
| Review and supervision | Mainstream — and creating an editing burden |
| Multiplayer | Essentially nonexistent; "massively parallel play" stayed unsocial |
| Attention | Exploded into agent workflows; creates intermittent-reward patterns |

### Mental models offered

- AI as **"an alien intelligence that studied humans"** — compared to the TV character Alf: sophisticated, well-briefed on us, still unpredictable.
- **"Sensitivity"** proposed as the successor concept to "taste": attunement to human needs, to product quality, and to teammate welfare.

### Concrete examples cited

- A researcher built a **fraud detector** independently when vendors could not deliver on the needed timeline.
- An internal **research collaborator** assistant holding context of all team research, with a shared skills repository.
- **Plusmaxgoone** — a weekly-updating catalog site built in thirty seconds.
- **Eval pipelines** that visibly display competitive performance against Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini.

## Stated Caveats

- **The jagged frontier persists.** Impressive results arrive inconsistently; people anthropomorphize on the strength of the good runs, then feel "betrayed" by the errors.
- **Novelty trapping.** Visually impressive implementations do not necessarily improve decisions.
- **"The tyranny of reviewing replaces the tyranny of the blank page."** Generation shifts the burden from writing to editing rather than removing it.
- **Agent workflows may be numbing** — abstraction layers plus intermittent reward, likened to "TikTok for productivity," risk emotional distance from the work.
- The primitives framework is **partially outdated** by the author's own assessment.

## Practical Recommendations

- Brief with intent, not step-by-step directives.
- Build in-house when vendors cannot move fast enough.
- Create psychological safety for experimentation explicitly.
- Maintain specialized expertise — the ability to code does not negate deep domain knowledge.
- Preserve human renewal; protect time away from the tools.
- Layer abstraction gradually as agent capability grows, to avoid cognitive overload.
