# Measuring the UX of AI — MeasuringU

**Authors:** Jeff Sauro, PhD; Jim Lewis, PhD
**Published:** 2026-07-28 · **Captured:** 2026-07-30
**Capture note:** AI-written summary. Full text not reproduced. Short quoted phrases retained for attribution.

## Summary

Sauro & Lewis propose extending standardized UX measurement to generative AI chat products. The approach is deliberately layered: start with **UX-Lite** for overall usefulness and usability, then add AI-specific constructs for diagnostic insight into *what* is driving those perceptions. They present an **initial 34-item questionnaire across six constructs** and state plainly that psychometric validation is still to come.

## Method framing

Mixed methods, split along a familiar axis:

- **Attitudinal measures** — standardized questionnaires; easier to collect.
- **Action / behavioral measures** — completion rates, errors, task time; more rigorous, harder to collect.

Recommended sequence: UX-Lite first for the high-level read, then specialized constructs to diagnose.

## The initial 34-item instrument (six constructs)

| Construct | Items | Example item |
|---|---:|---|
| **AI Productivity** | 8 | "Using this AI chatbot greatly improves my productivity"; "This AI chatbot's responses efficiently tell me the information I need" |
| **AI Trust** | 7 | "I trust this AI chatbot to provide reliable information" |
| **AI Dependency** | 3 | "I tend to accept answers from AI chatbots without verifying their accuracy" |
| **AI Anxiety** | 7 | "The increasing use of AI makes me uneasy" |
| **AI Personification** | 6 | "Interacting with this AI chatbot feels like communicating with a human" |
| **Early Adoption** | 3 | "I like to experiment with new technologies before most people do" |

Total: 8 + 7 + 3 + 7 + 6 + 3 = **34 items**. Item response format and scale anchors are **not stated** in the article. Psychometric development convention cited: minimum three items per construct.

## Context statistics cited (all from other sources, not original data)

- **Trust:** surveys show "less than half of people regularly using AI were willing to trust it."
- **Dependency:** **66%** rely on AI output without verifying accuracy; **56%** made mistakes from uncritical acceptance.
- Microsoft–LinkedIn 2024: **90%** reported time savings.
- Anthropic survey 2026: **86%** reported work speed improvements.
- Melbourne–KPMG survey: **48,000** respondents across **47** countries.
- AP-NORC 2025: **44%** believed AI would hurt society more than help.
- Prior related work on measuring website clutter (IJHCI paper).

## What is new versus existing measurement

**New:** six AI-specific constructs; an attempt to bridge high-level UX-Lite scores and granular diagnostic measurement for generative chat.
**Reused:** UX-Lite for usefulness/usability; standard psychometric development practice.

## Recommendations

1. Begin with UX-Lite for baseline usefulness and usability.
2. Add specialized constructs to diagnose which aspects drive perceptions.
3. Validate the proposed items through psychometric analysis.
4. Examine connections between the new constructs and higher-level outcomes — brand attitude, intention to continue use, recommendation intent.

## Caveats and limitations (largely stated by the authors)

- **Preliminary.** Item validation through psychometric evaluation is explicitly pending.
- **Construct validity untested.** The authors' own caution: "Items that look sensible on paper don't always hold up once real people respond."
- **No empirical data in this article.** The 34-item set is theoretically derived. No factor structure, reliability coefficients, or convergent validity reported.
- **Scope is chat-based generative AI.** May not generalize to recommendation algorithms, fraud detection, or other AI applications.
- **Scale format unspecified** in what was captured — an obstacle to reuse as-is.
- Future work committed: (1) factor structure validation, (2) item quality and retention decisions, (3) relationship analyses to external outcomes.
