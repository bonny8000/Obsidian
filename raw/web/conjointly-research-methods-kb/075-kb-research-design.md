## Design

Research design provides the glue that holds the research project together. A design is used to structure the research, to show how all of the major parts of the research project — the samples or groups, measures, treatments or programs, and methods of assignment — work together to try to address the central research questions. Here, after a brief [introduction to research design](https://conjointly.com/kb/introduction-to-design/), I’ll show you how we classify the major [types of designs](https://conjointly.com/kb/research-design-types/). You’ll see that a major distinction is between the [experimental designs](https://conjointly.com/kb/experimental-design/) that use random assignment to groups or programs and the [quasi-experimental designs](https://conjointly.com/kb/quasi-experimental-design/) that don’t use random assignment. \[People often confuse what is meant by random selection with the idea of random assignment. You should make sure that you understand the [distinction between random selection and random assignment](https://conjointly.com/kb/random-selection-and-assignment/).\] Understanding the [relationships among designs](https://conjointly.com/kb/pre-post-design-relationships/) is important in making design choices and thinking about the strengths and weaknesses of different designs. Then, I’ll talk about the heart of the art form of [designing designs for research](https://conjointly.com/kb/designing-research-designs/) and give you some ideas about how you can think about the design task. Finally, I’ll consider some of the more recent [advances in quasi-experimental thinking](https://conjointly.com/kb/quasi-experimentation-advances/) — an area of special importance in applied social research and program evaluation.
