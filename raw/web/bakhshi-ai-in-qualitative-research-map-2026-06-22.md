# AI in Qualitative Research: A Map, Not a Verdict

**Author:** Saeideh Bakhshi — Research Toolbox (Substack)
**Published:** 2026-06-04
**Subtitle:** "A framework for separating the uses, risks, and claims that get collapsed in the debate."

## Summary

Builds on her *Fallacy of Depth at Scale* (AI-assisted interviews) and the SAGE position paper rejecting GenAI in reflexive coding. Core argument: **"AI in qualitative research" is not one practice** — it names many different interventions (drafting guides, translating, chatbot interviews, cleaning transcripts, retrieval, applying a codebook, suggesting themes, second readings, drafting findings, quote selection, disclosure language). So "Does AI work in qual research?" is the wrong question; the literature looks contradictory only because studies ask different questions. She offers a **5-axis specification** to evaluate any AI-assisted qual work precisely.

## Key Points — the five specifications

1. **Stage** (where AI entered): before data collection / during data collection / after data collection / during analysis / during reporting. Earlier = shapes the frame or the data, higher stakes.
2. **Task type** (what work it did): operational, retrieval, classification (strongest evidence — deductive codebook application), summarization, exploratory, comparative (AI as a *second reading* — promising), interpretive (most unsettled), generative-of-data (AI interviewing — changes the data, not just analysis).
3. **Method fit** (does it match the tradition): deductive content analysis (fits well) vs reflexive thematic analysis (contested — themes are *developed* by positioned researchers, not discovered; Jowsey/Braun/Clarke + 414 signatories argue GenAI is not congruent), grounded theory, discourse analysis, phenomenology, ethnography, participatory — each has different assumptions.
4. **Output status** (what the output is allowed to count as): suggestion → working material → candidate analytic object → evidence pointer → draft claim → supported claim. Much damage = treating candidate outputs as findings.
5. **Validation & traceability** (how it was checked): traceability to source; fit-with-output-type checks; visible human revision; comparison across readings; negative-case checking; documentation of limits. "Human in the loop" is too vague unless you can see what the human did.

## What the literature suggests (high level)

- AI is most useful for **bounded, checkable tasks** (deductive coding ≈ human agreement); descriptive matching ≠ interpretive analysis.
- AI as a **second analytic reading** is the most methodologically promising use (compare convergence/divergence).
- Outputs vary by model/prompt/data/workflow (situated, not stable facts).
- AI-mediated interviews are promising but produce a *different kind of account*; under-studied.
- "Human review necessary but not sufficient as a phrase"; validation is becoming a design problem.

Includes a practical disclosure checklist (what AI was used for, where, what task, what method, output status, traceability, what humans changed, what was NOT delegated, model conditions, what claim is justified, limits).
