## Thesis

Upselling increases transaction value by offering a higher-value option after purchase intent already exists. It can be efficient when perceived customer value rises faster than marginal delivery cost, but the offer must preserve clear choice to avoid damaging trust.

## Three psychological routes

### 1. Value upsell

Offer more quantity, capability, or specification for a relatively small incremental cost. Examples include larger fast-food sides and higher phone storage. The customer responds to gain seeking: if they are already buying, a visibly better version can feel efficient.

### 2. Reassurance upsell

Sell protection against a future loss, such as an extended warranty, insurance add-on, or device protection plan. The customer buys reduced anxiety; the seller benefits when claims are infrequent. This route has a high risk of exploiting fear or information asymmetry.

### 3. Convenience upsell

Sell reduced waiting, effort, or friction without materially changing the core product: priority boarding, seat selection, extra baggage, direct delivery, or faster service. Time and effort become the upgraded product.

## Offer mechanics

- **Timing:** present the upgrade close to purchase confirmation, when intent is settled and the incremental price feels smaller than the base purchase.
- **Defaults:** preselection can raise take-up because people avoid changing defaults, but hidden or difficult opt-out turns an upsell into a dark pattern.
- **Unit economics:** focus on offers where willingness to pay exceeds marginal cost; calculate incremental margin, not only attachment rate.
- **Trust boundary:** explain the benefit, total price, and opt-out clearly. A short-term conversion lift does not justify surprise charges or coerced choice.

## Product questions

- Where in the journey has the user already committed to the base job?
- Which upgrade improves value, reassurance, or convenience without degrading the base option?
- Is the incremental price legible relative to incremental benefit?
- Can the customer decline in one obvious action?
- Does the metric set include refunds, complaints, retention, and trust, not only immediate revenue?

## Caveats

- This is an educational VC blog article, not a controlled study.
- Several industry figures are presented without primary-source citations in the extracted article.
- Near-zero marginal cost for a specific example does not mean the offer has zero operational, support, or reputational cost.
- Default-on offers require legal, ethical, and accessibility review in the relevant market.
