# UXR, Market Research, and Data Science Walk Into a Reorg — Constantine Papas

**Published:** 2026-07-13 · **Captured:** 2026-07-28
**Capture note:** AI-written summary with short quoted phrases for attribution. Full text not reproduced. Opinion/prediction essay, part of a series.

## Summary

A prediction that **UX Research, Market Research, and user-facing Data Science merge into one discipline within roughly three years** — driven not by management restructuring but by AI collapsing the technical barriers between them and by the emergence of a shared, dated, confidence-weighted knowledge base about users.

## Argument about org structure

**The walls are accidental.** The author traces current divisions to lineage rather than design: usability/HCI for UXR, marketing for market research, instrumentation/engineering for data science. Each calcified around distinct data streams and reporting lines. Framing: **"Nobody ever decided that understanding customers should be split three ways. It accreted, hire by hire."**

**Why earlier mergers failed.** The "insights department" consolidations common in the 2010s changed reporting lines without addressing **locality of interpretation** — teams kept analyzing their own data streams through separate interpretive frames, producing siloed conclusions that never synthesized.

## Key claims

1. **Technical democratization.** AI collapsed the technical barrier across the three disciplines. Practitioners "vibe code" tools, reason about system architecture, and ship prototypes without formal engineering backgrounds, so they can traverse formerly separate domains.
2. **Pre-existing proximity.** The three fields already sit on a technical-to-less-technical spectrum with overlapping backgrounds (math-heavy social science, CS, HCI). **"The fence was mostly tooling friction, and the friction is exactly what the models dissolved."**
3. **Shared state is the merger mechanism.** A unified, dated, confidence-weighted knowledge base about users makes separate interpretation gates incoherent. Intelligence-community assessment practice — unified interpretation despite distinct collection disciplines — is offered as the functional model.
4. **The practitioners are building it.** The hybrid individuals traversing all three functions are simultaneously constructing the shared-state infrastructure that will absorb their own distinct roles.

## Timeline and its basis

**Prediction:** roughly three years to functional merger, hedged on whether *all* of data science merges. **Basis:** two independently converging forces — individual practitioner capability expansion and shared-state infrastructure — creating pressure that reporting-line mergers cannot resist because they never addressed root causes.

## Two outcomes distinguished

- **Cost-driven ("the memo version"):** consolidation as headcount reduction, with interpretation responsibility orphaned to whatever tool is nearest.
- **Capability-driven:** distinct collection crafts stay plural while assessment unifies, producing coherent organizational knowledge.

They "look identical in the announcement and nothing alike three years later."

## Practical implications

- Narrow disciplinary expertise compounds into disadvantage as technical fluency becomes prerequisite.
- Technical practitioners gain an arbitrage advantage through capability expansion.
- Advancement depends less on disciplinary depth, more on interpretation-framework fluency.
- Organizations gain unified customer understanding but risk losing craft quality in cost-focused implementations.

## Opinion versus evidence

**Opinion (no supporting data):** that the merger will occur; the three-year horizon; the intelligence-community analogy as the right organizational model; that the capability-driven version is preferable.

**Observable / defensible:** prior mergers failed to eliminate silos; AI tools have lowered technical barriers; hybrid practitioners already exist and cross disciplines.

## Caveats and explicit uncertainty

The author defers to later installments: how shared state is actually built and maintained (part 3), whether user-facing data science fully merges or partially stays in engineering, and why organizational leadership structure should be personal rather than supervisory. The author also flags self-suspicion about their own certainty — noting they repeatedly tried and failed to construct a version where the walls survive contact with shared state, and that the certainty survived the suspicion.
