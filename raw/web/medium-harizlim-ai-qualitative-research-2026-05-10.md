## Core Argument

Challenges the common practice of using AI primarily to generate thematic summaries from qualitative research data. Lim argues that automated thematic summarization is the "lowest-value task an AI can do." The real value lies in using AI to enhance human thinking and reasoning — as a "structured thinking partner" — while humans retain responsibility for interpretation and meaning-making.

---

## Five Main Points

1. **Move Beyond Binary Thinking** — The human-vs-AI debate is outdated. Researchers work alongside AI iteratively rather than handing analysis off completely.

2. **AI as Co-Consultant, Not Analyst** — AI excels at processing language and surfacing patterns but lacks contextual understanding. Lim uses AI to pressure-test insights against evidence rather than to generate initial findings.

3. **Humans Own Meaning** — Researchers bring irreplaceable context: observed moments, stakeholder dynamics, what remained unspoken, and real-world constraints that AI cannot access independently.

4. **Quality Depends on the Researcher** — Strong AI integration demands rigorous input, multiple perspectives, and ruthless interrogation of outputs. Weak use (dumping raw data) creates false credibility without substance.

5. **Reflexivity Intensifies** — Responsible AI use forces clearer articulation of reasoning, exposes biases, and serves as a sparring partner that sharpens thinking rather than undermining reflexive practice.

---

## Practical Example

Research on the Healthy 365 health-tracking app: users appeared to overlook horizontal carousel scrolling. Rather than accepting this surface observation, Lim used AI to systematically review participant verbatims and video evidence. Uncovered a more nuanced truth: users consciously preferred direct-access shortcuts over exploratory browsing, revealing conditional trust across multiple health platforms — not simple usability confusion.

---

## Critical Warning

Deploying AI without expertise creates the illusion of rigor while potentially derailing strategic decisions. Organizations that replace junior researchers with AI risk eroding the talent pipeline needed to work effectively with these tools.

**Key quote:** "AI doesn't replace reflexive practice — it makes it harder to get away with bad thinking."
