## Capture Notes (paraphrased, not verbatim)

18 design guidelines synthesized from 150+ recommendations across two decades of industry and academic guidance, validated through expert reviews plus a study with 49 practitioners testing the guidelines against 20 AI products. Motivation: classic guidelines like consistency break down for AI components that learn and change over time.

The guidelines are organized into four interaction phases (guideline numbering G1-G18; phrasing below paraphrased):
- Initially (G1-G2): set expectations — communicate what the system can do and how well it does it.
- During interaction (G3-G6): be context-aware — time services to the user's situation, surface contextually relevant information, fit social norms, and mitigate social biases.
- When wrong (G7-G11): design for failure — make the service easy to invoke, easy to dismiss, easy to correct; reduce scope or ask when uncertain; explain why the system behaved as it did.
- Over time (G12-G18): manage the relationship — remember recent interactions, learn from behavior, change cautiously, invite granular feedback, convey consequences of user actions, provide global controls, and announce changes.

Practical artifacts: guideline cards and the Microsoft HAX Toolkit (design patterns + playbook per guideline).
