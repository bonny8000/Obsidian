# Bayes' Law in UX Research: The Power and Perils of Priors

**Authors:** Jeff Sauro, PhD and Jim Lewis, PhD
**Published:** 2026-03-31 — MeasuringU

## Overview

The article examines how prior beliefs in Bayesian analysis significantly influence UX research conclusions. Using a case study of a **90% completion rate from 20 participants**, the authors demonstrate that the same observed data can lead to different conclusions depending on the priors selected.

## Key finding

The same 18-of-20 task completion scenario produced varying conclusions across five prior scenarios:

- With **neutral priors:** 90% completion rate is 2.7× more likely.
- With **strong priors favoring historical 78%:** 78% becomes more likely.
- With **strong priors favoring 90%:** 90% is 10.9× more likely.

> "Changing the prior assumption had a larger effect on the conclusion than a modest increase in sample size would."

## Main sections

**The effect of priors on the outcome.** Prior beliefs represent plausible explanations before analyzing current data. Researchers assign numerical weights to competing hypotheses.

**How our conclusions change based on priors.** Four of five scenarios favored 90%, but only the strong 78%-favoring prior reversed this conclusion.

## Recommendations for researchers

- Be explicit about prior selection methodology.
- Conduct prior sensitivity analysis.
- Exercise caution with uncertain priors.
- Collect additional data when conclusions heavily depend on assumptions.

The article emphasizes using Bayesian methods "thoughtfully and transparently" rather than avoiding them entirely.
