## Source summary

The author reports that Microsoft Forms produced a questionable winner in a 32-person force-ranking exercise. He exported the underlying responses and compared the default result with Borda scoring, pairwise wins, and the Schulze method. The practical lesson is to validate the aggregation logic of third-party research tools instead of treating a polished default chart as ground truth.

## Reusable evidence

- A ranking tool can make an analytic choice invisible to the researcher.
- Exporting raw responses enables an independent check of the default winner.
- Alternative rank aggregation methods can produce materially different outcomes.

## Limits

The source is a practitioner case report, not an independent benchmark of Microsoft Forms or a general proof that one ranking method is best.
