## Summary

Pearson proposes a practical self-study path for quantitative UX research. He starts from the common framing that quant UXR requires UX research, statistics, and programming, then splits UX research into two more teachable pillars: surveys and research design. The resulting learning model has four pillars: statistics, surveys, programming, and research design.

The guide is aimed at qualitative researchers, students, and mixed-methods UX researchers who want to build more quantitative capability. It emphasizes that there is no single degree path and that many quant UXRs learn through a mix of formal training, self-study, and job-based practice.

## Four Pillars

| Pillar | Meaning in the guide |
| --- | --- |
| Statistics | Confidence intervals, analysis logic, statistical interpretation, and data visualization |
| Surveys | Survey design, sampling, measurement error, wording, response options, and survey operations |
| Programming | R, Python, SQL, data cleaning, analysis, and visualization |
| Research design | Overall strategy for reducing bias, increasing validity, and choosing an appropriate method |

## Self-study Routes Captured

- For a fast survey need, Pearson recommends starting with practical survey guidance and confidence intervals.
- For qualitative researchers learning surveys, the guide recommends moving from intuitive question-writing to empirically tested survey design and statistical analysis.
- For students, it recommends courses with hands-on survey design or statistical analysis projects.
- For mixed-methods researchers, it argues they may already have relevant foundations and should choose the weakest pillar first.
- The programming advice is pragmatic: R is useful for data cleaning, statistical analysis, and visualization; Python has broader domains; SQL is useful because large quantitative data often lives in databases.

## Extracted Claims

- Quant UXR learning is more manageable when broken into explicit pillars.
- Surveys deserve special attention because they appear consistently in quant UXR job expectations and practice.
- Research design is separate from survey execution because it governs bias, validity, and whether a method fits the question.
- Programming is best learned against a concrete analysis goal rather than as abstract syntax.
- Quant UXR does not necessarily require machine learning, Bayesian statistics, or advanced modeling at the entry point.
