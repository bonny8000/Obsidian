## Selected Post: Rigor in Quant UX Research

URL: https://quantuxblog.com/rigor-in-quant-ux-research

Published: 2026-02-03

Observed author: Chris Chapman

Key extraction:

- Rigor is not equivalent to advanced methods, p-values, massive data sets, or rigid protocols.
- The proposed rigor stack has four levels: focus on learning and decisions; quality data and methods; effective stakeholder engagement; and attention to higher-order strategic decisions.
- Decision process should exist before research starts so findings know what action, follow-up, or tradeoff they are meant to inform.
- Quant UXR quality comes from appropriate learning under uncertainty, not from method complexity alone.

## Selected Post: Synthetic Survey Data? It's Not Data

URL: https://quantuxblog.com/synthetic-survey-data-its-not-data

Published: 2025-06-18

Observed author: Chris Chapman

Key extraction:

- The post argues that LLM-generated survey responses should not be treated as human survey data.
- It gives three central objections: the concept misunderstands surveys as motivated human communication; empirical comparisons show weak alignment with humans; and synthetic data cannot solve sampling limitations.
- The post cites empirical warning signs from Bisbee et al. 2024, Paxton and Yang 2024, and Samoylov 2024.
- One reported Paxton and Yang snapshot found low correlations between LLM and human attitudes, with a median correlation around r = 0.10 across the assessed attitudes.
- The practical implication is that synthetic survey data may be useful for some tool tests or thought experiments, but it should not replace listening to actual users or customers.

## Extracted Claims

- Quant UXR sources should be evaluated for decision usefulness, data quality, and methodological fit, not only statistical sophistication.
- Learning and decision framing come before method choice.
- AI-generated or synthetic user data should not be used as a substitute for human evidence without strong validity justification.
- Quant and qual methods can strengthen each other when they answer complementary pieces of the same decision.
