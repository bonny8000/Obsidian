## Collection Notes

- The article is a position statement from 419 experienced qualitative researchers from 32 countries.
- The authors argue that reflexive qualitative approaches, including reflexive thematic analysis, require subjective, positioned, reflexive human researchers.
- They reject GenAI use for reflexive qualitative approaches on methodological congruence grounds and on social/environmental justice grounds.
- The article is useful as a counterweight to AI-for-research-automation sources.

## Extracted Claims

- Reflexive thematic analysis is not just coding or pattern extraction; it depends on positioned human interpretation.
- GenAI use may be methodologically incongruent with Big Q qualitative approaches.
- AI research workflows should be evaluated for epistemic, ethical, environmental, and justice implications, not only productivity.
