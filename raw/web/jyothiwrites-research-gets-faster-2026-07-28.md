# When Research Gets Faster, Why Do We Need Researchers? — Jyothi Venkat

**Published:** 2026-07-27 · **Captured:** 2026-07-28
**Capture note:** AI-written summary of an opinion essay. Full text not reproduced.

## Summary

An argument that as AI accelerates research execution, the profession's value must relocate from **conducting studies** to **influencing decisions**. Researchers remain necessary not because machines cannot analyze data, but because organizations are bad at upstream problem framing and downstream accountability — neither of which is what got automated.

## Key claims

1. **Research begins before data arrives.** The critical work is clarifying the decision, surfacing assumptions, and determining what evidence could realistically change the outcome. This is rarely automated.
2. **Volume creates noise, not insight.** More studies do not improve decision quality; organizations already struggle to prioritize conflicting signals.
3. **Interpretation requires judgment.** Separating signal from noise depends on business context, product dynamics, and risk tolerance.
4. **Research is an organizational system.** Methodologically sound findings still fail when they lack ownership, face competing priorities, or arrive after a decision is already committed.

## Examples used

- Interview-transcript analysis tools as the demonstration of AI's mechanical capability.
- Feature-preference testing run *after* architecture was finalized.
- Onboarding-abandonment research where the causes were already known.
- Trust studies conducted ahead of a launch decision that was already made.

## Tensions identified

- **Efficiency versus effectiveness.** Faster research may expose organizational dysfunction rather than fix it.
- **Automation risk.** Replacing analytical labor without redefining research's strategic function can *reduce* influence even as speed improves.

## Practical implications

- Move from study execution to decision partnering.
- Invest time in assumption validation before research begins.
- Track outcomes post-launch to demonstrate business impact.
- Design organizational systems that give findings ownership and accountability.

## Caveats

The piece does not address resource constraints in under-resourced teams, the mechanics of implementing upstream/downstream engagement, or organizational resistance to expanding research influence. It is argument, not evidence — no study data is presented.
