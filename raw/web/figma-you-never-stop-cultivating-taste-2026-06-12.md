## Summary

Loredana Crisan argues that craft mastery is not only a matter of acquiring skills or tools. Expertise also depends on cultivating a point of view: a way of seeing, judging, and expressing intent. AI does not remove this requirement. If anything, it makes taste more important because generated output still needs discernment, care, and repeated intentional choices.

The article frames taste as care for the craft, care for the user, and care for the final result. Taste shows up in trade-offs, details, critique, collaboration, empathy, and the refusal to accept the first output as final.

## Extracted Claims

- Mastery requires practice, feedback, mentors, and sustained attention, not just tools.
- Taste is a cultivated point of view plus the skill to express it.
- Taste can be visible in trade-offs such as form vs. function, expressivity vs. legibility, and what to add vs. remove.
- Taste starts with love for craft and care for the people being reached.
- Design critique and collaboration are important because taste develops socially, not in a vacuum.
- Hiring for taste can look for discernment, empathy, and creative energy.
- AI can broaden exploration and reduce the gap between imagination and execution, but it cannot replace the designer's taste.
- Accepting first AI output as final is a failure mode for craft judgment.
