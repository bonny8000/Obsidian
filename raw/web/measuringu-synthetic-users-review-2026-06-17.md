# A Review of Experiments with Synthetic Users

**Authors:** Jim Lewis, PhD and Jeff Sauro, PhD
**Published:** 2026-04-14 — MeasuringU

## Summary

This article examines 12 peer-reviewed research papers comparing synthetic users (AI-generated via LLMs) with human participants across four research contexts relevant to UX professionals.

## Key findings

9 encouraging findings, 14 discouraging findings across studies. Main issues with synthetic users:

- **Reduced variance** in responses compared to humans
- **Superficial agreement** on high-level metrics but errors in deeper analysis
- **Systematic distortions** such as exaggerated effects
- **Shallow qualitative responses** lacking lived experience
- **Potential bias issues** in representing diverse groups

## Research categories reviewed

1. **Psychological Experiments (5 papers).** Only 21% successfully replicated classic studies.
2. **Surveys (3 papers).** Means sometimes matched, but subgroup means, standard deviations, and regression coefficients were inaccurate.
3. **Social Research (3 papers).** Directional trends matched humans, but deeper attitudinal variance showed weak correspondence.
4. **UX Interviews (1 paper).** Initial narratives seemed promising, but fundamental limitations emerged during extended conversations.

## Recommendations

Synthetic users show promise primarily for "deriving insights from already collected data" rather than generating novel research findings. The authors caution against relying on synthetic user research for critical decision-making until stronger evidence of consistent human-data matching emerges.
