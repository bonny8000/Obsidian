# How AI Reads the World — The Input-Modality Competition on Wearables

**Author:** Kakao Ventures (editor: Chloe) — kakao.vc blog, 2026-06-24.
**Capture status:** Fetched via web_fetch 2026-06-25; AI-written summary (not verbatim). A VC investment-thesis piece.

## Summary

As AI takes on more decision-making, competitive advantage shifts from **model performance → contextual understanding**. **Wearables** become critical infrastructure because they capture signals smartphones can't — gaze, motion, biometrics. The narrative is a transition from **explicit, user-initiated input** to **continuous, embodied data collection**. The investment frontier is *how* technology enters the body and daily life, not raw sensor accuracy.

## Key Points

- **Definition — Input Modality:** the format through which AI receives information: text, image, voice, and sensor-derived signals (eye gaze, movement, neural activity).
- **Taxonomy by signal channel:**
  1. **Auditory** — voice + ambient sound; commercially mature, but captures limited state beyond what the user chooses to express.
  2. **Visual** — camera + eye-tracking; rich environmental context + attention, but battery, thermal, and third-party privacy are hard.
  3. **Motion** — accelerometer/gyroscope/IMU; intuitive control, but must separate intentional commands from everyday movement.
  4. **Physiological / Neural** — heart rate, temperature, EMG, EEG, BCI; captures undisclosed states; high sensitivity, hard to interpret.
- **Taxonomy by collection mechanism:**
  1. **Explicit input** — user-initiated (button, voice activation); high control, but limits continuous context.
  2. **Standby mode** — device watches for a trigger (wake word, gesture); less friction, but awareness/consent ambiguity.
  3. **Continuous measurement** — passive accumulation (smartwatch, ring); enables temporal understanding, but raises control/trust concerns.
- **Investment thesis — three barriers to viability:** (1) **Technical performance** — stable signal interpretation across conditions; (2) **Physical adoption** — daily wearability via design, weight, battery, aesthetics; (3) **Data trust** — consent structures for personal *and third-party* data.
- **Positioning:** winning wearables behave as **fashion accessories** (Ray-Ban Meta, Oura Ring) while guaranteeing privacy (on-device processing, transparency indicators, user control over storage/retention).
- **Examples:** Google Glass (failed — privacy + design unfamiliarity despite feasibility); Meta Ray-Ban (AI sensing inside an established fashion brand); Oura Ring (biometrics in a familiar form); Apple Vision Pro (mature eye-tracking).
- **Key quote:** "The winner will not be the technology that collects the most data, but the one that enables users to naturally adopt it while safely utilizing their information."
