## Capture Notes (paraphrased, not verbatim)

Review paper integrating trust research across organizational, sociological, interpersonal, psychological, and neurological perspectives into a conceptual model of human-automation trust. Core problem framing: automation failures often stem from inappropriate reliance rather than system capability alone. People respond to technology socially, so trust governs reliance especially when systems are too complex to fully understand.

Framework elements as described in the paper and secondary literature:
- Failure taxonomy: misuse (over-reliance on automation beyond its capability) and disuse (under-reliance, rejecting capable automation).
- Three target properties for trust: calibration (trust level matches actual capability), resolution (trust differentiates between contexts/functions where capability differs), and specificity (trust attaches to the right component or function rather than the whole system).
- Trust formation operates through three information dimensions about the automation: purpose (why it exists, designer intent), process (how it works), and performance (what it does, track record).
- Trust forms through analytic, analogical, and affective cognitive routes; affect matters more than purely rational models assume.
- Design implication: display and interface characteristics shape trust dynamics; the goal is designing for appropriate reliance, not maximizing trust.

~4,000+ citations; foundational reference for later appropriate-reliance work on AI and LLMs (e.g., arXiv 2311.06305 systematic review; arXiv 2412.15584 on LLM reliance interventions).
