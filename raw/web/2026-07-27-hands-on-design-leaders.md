## Source summary

The article argues for a player-coach form of design leadership: leaders who can still enter the work, unblock teams, and make concrete decisions while also creating outcomes through other people. It distinguishes hands-on leadership from both pure individual contribution and detached people management.

## Reusable evidence

- Senior leadership credibility can depend on maintaining enough craft fluency to inspect and unblock real work.
- The leadership question shifts from “can you do the work?” to “can you make good work happen through other people?”
- Hands-on leadership is a role design and context question, not a mandate for leaders to absorb every execution task.

## Limits

This is leadership advice and commentary, not a labor-market study.
